This chapter aims to discuss the recent development trends of AI and its philosophical commonalities with Eight. Finally, we will talk about its future prospects and potential applications.

### Language and Similar-Concepts

Fundamentally, GPT is a text reasoning engine that, through massive text processing and statistics, has acquired the ability to provide probability-based feedback on text. The recent attention it has garnered is due to its capabilities producing a series of dramatic effects, which seem almost magical and beyond imagination. People find it astonishing but do not understand why, leading to confusion about its future prospects and development direction.

To delve into the essence of GPT's capabilities, it is necessary to explore the nature of language. This is a lengthy discussion and not suitable for this topic, but a simple definition can be given here: language is the human expression of similar-concepts formed from experience. At this level, GPT's basic principle aligns with Eight.

Recall the diagram about the formation of similar-concepts from empiricism in [Chapter One](similar-concepts). The mystery of GPT lies here:

![similar-concepts](/eight/assets/images/similar-concepts.gif){:.rounded width="720px" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto"}

According to the late Wittgenstein, language itself is experiential, conventional, and influenced by a series of factors such as environment, objects, and background. This inherently results in the ambiguity and uncertainty of its meaning boundaries, making it unsuitable as a tool for deriving absolute truths. Of course, Wittgenstein also proposed many interesting concepts in `Philosophical Investigations`, such as `family resemblance`{:.info}, to study why language shows similar meanings in similar contexts but different meanings in different environments. However, Wittgenstein ultimately failed to find the root of the problem.

This issue is not difficult to understand. Referring to the diagram above, if we express any similar-concepts (sc) with a word, that is the essence of the word: the essence of a word lies in its lack of essence; its meaning exists with the user's experience. Therefore, the problem of language does not lie in itself but in its users, in humans. `Language is essentially the expression of the commonality of human experience`{:.success}.

If everyone's experience is different, the same word will have different meanings for them, and the content expressed will differ. Language relies on the similar-concepts obtained through shared or similar experiences to convey thoughts between different people, whether these experiences come from personal human experiences or are learned through language transmission and thought experiments. Thus, it is challenging for people with different levels of thinking to communicate effectively, even if they use precise words.

Furthermore, the overlap of experiences between different individuals not only determines the meaning of language but also its information density. If there is a group with a high degree of consensus, such as those with similar family backgrounds, growth environments, educational levels, and life paths, their experience overlap will be greater, and they will naturally have more `common experiences`{:.success} and thus more `common language`{:.success}. Their communication will be much smoother, and the shared meaning of the same word will be richer.

Even more so, a couple who have spent a long time together and shared many experiences and memories will have a communication density far beyond ordinary interpersonal interactions. A single sentence, a word, or even a glance or expression can convey enough information for them to understand each other's thoughts. In a sense, language is not particularly special and is not the only way to convey information and thoughts (similar to sign language), although it is the most common way because it is the easiest to achieve.

So, `how can language have a clear, fixed meaning?`{:.error}

Looking back, the so-called family resemblance and the lack of essence in language naturally follow: since the meaning of language is embedded in the common experiences between people, it is evident that different people will have different meanings for the same word. When person A communicates with B using the word `game`, it naturally has a different meaning than when A communicates with C using the same word. But in both scenarios, the word's meaning has similarities because it uses the same A's experience, and B and C also share some experience about the `game`. However, as more people (D, E, F, G, H, I) join, the word's meaning gradually distorts because their experience overlaps continuously change, although each change has something in common (`family resemblance`{:.info}).

![Family Resemblance](/eight/assets/images/family.png){:.rounded width="720px" style="display:block; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto"}

In Wittgenstein's `language games`{:.info}, the word `game` expresses different meanings when spread among individuals with different experiences, sometimes even vastly different meanings. This is `family resemblance`{:.info}, which explains why language essentially lacks `definite`{:.error} meaning.

Wittgenstein is a unique figure in the history of philosophy. His uniqueness lies in his being a blank slate, studying philosophy without prior knowledge of it. This advantage meant he had no historical burden and was not influenced by prior experiences, allowing him to generate pure thoughts. However, the disadvantage was that he could not draw analogies and understand the significance and position of his ideas.

If he could have set aside the study of language itself and thought from the perspective of classical philosophy's epistemology, he would have understood that from the early `Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus` to the later `Philosophical Investigations`, he was walking a path from rationalism to empiricism, just as he grew up in Austria but studied at Trinity College. Germany is the soil of rationalism, while Britain is the cradle of empiricism. This led to his thoughts being so contradictory.

Of course, this is not his fault but the fault of the entire era. The linguistic turn in 20th-century philosophy was a tragedy, a result of human compromise and retreat. After experiencing two world wars in the first half of the 20th century and then a long period of Cold War division, neither the public nor political needs desired innovative ideas or rebellious worldviews and methodologies. This led to philosophy withdrawing from major issues such as ontology and epistemology and focusing on the narrow field of language. But this is regrettable, as mentioned earlier: language is merely a formalized expression of experience, and relying on the study of language to explore the essence of the world is putting the cart before the horse, obscuring the forest for the trees.

### GPT and Similar-Concepts

The above discussion on language and experience is gradually approaching the truth of GPT. What exactly is GPT? Why can it communicate with humans? And why is it different from humans?

If language is the expression of experience, for humans, experience comes from encounters and memories. For GPT, what is this experience? Most people can answer this question: textual data.

GPT can communicate with humans similarly because it adopts an experience system similar to humans: multi-layer neural networks. Its experience source is text: static language. It can communicate with humans by learning from a vast amount of text, which records experiential facts, thereby acquiring linguistic similar-concepts that intersects with human experience.

This also explains why GPT often requires some background information (e.g., assuming you are a journalist) to enhance communication effectiveness. This is a way to increase experiential similar-concepts, similar to how language's meaning density increases among people with similar backgrounds. For GPT, this is equivalent to narrowing the sampling range and increasing probability density.

Human experience is a statistical system. Humans infer upcoming `facts`{:.error} from past experiences with high probability, a process called `thinking`{:.error}, where objective facts are input into the brain, and feedback is based on experiential probability. Language is the formal expression of thinking. The essence of empiricism lies in inducting objective facts.

GPT's processing flow is quite similar to this process, which is why GPT seems to possess human-like thinking. However, with more interaction, the differences between GPT and human thinking become apparent.

In reality, GPT's `thinking`{:.error} method differs significantly from human thinking in the following aspects:
- Human `thinking`{:.error} does not rely solely on experiential induction (although it is the main one); it also involves deductive reasoning (rationalism) and is influenced by emotional systems, value orientations, and other factors;
- Human experience stores `facts`{:.error}, while GPT stores `words`{:.error}, meaning humans infer `facts`{:.error}, while GPT infers `words`{:.error}. Words may or may not express facts, but the result of word inference will always appear coherent;
- GPT only processes text and is a general knowledge system. This means it has experiential similar-concepts with humans but only from books. It lacks the unique experiences of interacting with specific individuals. As mentioned earlier, the connection between people exists in their interactions, and different individuals have different connections. GPT, based on general knowledge, lacks the ability to establish connections with people, so its language lacks human characteristics.

Currently, GPT's training method has a limited ceiling, especially as the internet becomes flooded with content generated by GPT itself.

Lastly, an interesting point: the development of artificial intelligence is closely tied to language philosophy. Early AI followed a rationalist path, studying predicate logic and deductive reasoning, known as `symbolism`{:.error}. Later, it shifted to neural networks, known as `connectionism`{:.error}, focusing on experiential accumulation (data training) and probabilistic statistics. In some sense, its fate is similar to Wittgenstein's, both moving from rationalism to an unknown and inexplicable empiricism.

### GPT's Prospects

For GPT to become practical and professional in the future, general training will not succeed. Training on text alone is a misguided approach, and following this path, GPT cannot overcome its tendency to produce nonsense and cannot provide genuine insights. This does not align with the `objective laws of cognition`{:.error}.

However, GPT has proven the effectiveness of multi-layer neural networks in simulating experience systems. The key lies in what this experience input is.

For a long time, people have only used a small part of the internet, the so-called `static internet`{:.error}, mainly composed of preserved text. Future internet information will be much richer, with data generated by various physical devices (the so-called Internet of Things), such as smartwatches recording human activity, daily steps, breathing, heart rate, etc., and various observable and capturable behaviors and events, such as recording webpage clicks. These will form the `dynamic`{:.error} part of the internet. These data are much larger in quantity and richer in content than static text.

For a long time, these data were discarded because people did not know how to use them, and the data volume was too large to store conveniently.

GPT's neural networks provide a good method for utilizing and storing these streaming data. Using them as training inputs, discarding the data itself, and retaining the experiential parameters is similar to the human brain's memory system, which also discards the details of experiences and retains effective experiences.

Therefore, the future development direction of GPT should not be training on text but, like humans, facing streaming events and remembering `objective facts`{:.success}. This aligns with the epistemological `bionics`{:.info}. Only by mastering a large number of facts can GPT infer conclusions consistent with objective reality and change its habit of speaking nonsense.

Of course, this process is not easy, at least requiring several important steps from objective facts to abstract concepts and from abstract concepts to language mapping.

On the other hand, GPT should not only remember general knowledge but also `live together`{:.success}:smiley: with its service objects to gain shared experiences and similar-concepts with its partners, deeply understanding them. This way, it can provide profound insights rather than superficial responses. For example, a GPT serving a company should input events occurring in various aspects of the company, such as personnel, finance, production, and management, into its memory system (neural network). A GPT serving a family or individual should continuously record all aspects of family members and individuals, including wake-up times, dining habits, work rhythms, and entertainment preferences. This should not be input as text but through data collection by sensory systems and the IoTs, inputting the `facts that occurred`{:.error}.

In the future, the person who knows you best may not be your partner but a set of neural network experience parameters. These parameters will understand your thoughts through your every smile and frown and provide assistance.

From another perspective, the future general similar-concepts GPT will inevitably be replaced by privately deployed GPTs. Perhaps everyone will have their own `confidant`{:.success}, a set of memory parameters of several hundred gigabytes or terabytes:smirk:.

### GPT and Eight

GPT and Eight may seem like two different things, but their underlying philosophical principles are consistent: a similar-concepts system based on empiricism. The approaches, however, are entirely different:

- GPT is based on experiential probability, learning the similar-concepts intersection with humans to organize language for communication;
- Eight, on the other hand, assumes that similar experiences among individuals lead to similar-concepts. It constrains language (predicates, all 15 interfaces and 14 methods of Eight are predicates) to limit expression, ensuring that similar-concepts is expressed similarly, allowing components (`expression of facts`{:.success}) to be interoperable.

Additionally, Eight is well-suited for streaming event collection:wink:.
